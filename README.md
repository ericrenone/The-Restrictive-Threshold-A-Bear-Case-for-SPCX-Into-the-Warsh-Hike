**Baseline:** $SPCX ~$144.77, down ~36% from the June 16 peak of $225.64, ~65x run-rate sales. FOMC decision today, 2:00 PM ET, with a ~91–93% market-implied probability of the first rate hike since 2023, to 3.75–4.00%. 10-year Treasury near 5.0%. ## The Claim

Today the Federal Reserve is expected to raise interest rates for the first time in three years, into a level its own framework already calls insufficiently restrictive, driven by an energy shock that twenty-five basis points cannot touch. That single sentence contains the entire bear case for the most expensive, longest-duration, most narrative-dependent large-capitalization equity in the market. $SPCX is not merely exposed to this decision — it is the purest expression of everything the decision threatens: a ~65x-sales valuation whose worth lives almost entirely in cash flows a decade out, priced for a low-rate world, meeting a Fed that has just told markets the low-rate world is over and there is "work to do."

This document makes the bear case in its ordinary, unsensational form. It does not require a catastrophe. It requires only that a hiking cycle begin as expected, that an energy shock persist as the physics dictate, that a hard launch carry ordinary risk, that a wall of supply arrive on schedule, and that a valuation with no floor be asked, for the first time in its public life, to justify itself without the tailwind of cheap money. Each of those is the consensus expectation. The bear case is simply the observation that they arrive together, in a single window, against a stock that has already fallen 36% and still trades at a multiple that assumes none of them.

## Part I: The Decision and Why It Lands Hardest Here

### The hike itself is not the danger — the threshold is

The 25-basis-point move is the most telegraphed decision in years. An 85% majority of economists — 86 of 101 in the post-CPI Reuters survey — expect it, and market-implied odds sit near 91–93%. That consensus is precisely why the hike alone is nearly inert as a catalyst: a fully anticipated action, once taken, changes little. The danger is the sentence buried in the mechanics of it. One 25-basis-point move takes the federal funds rate to 3.75–4.00% — a level the Fed has already characterized as insufficiently restrictive. If that logic holds, September is a starting point, not a destination. The rate that clears today is, by the committee's own standard, not high enough to do the job. That is the definition of a cycle, not an event.

### Why a duration asset cannot absorb a cycle the way it can absorb an event

Every equity is a claim on future cash flows discounted to today. The further out the cash flows, the more violently their present value moves when the discount rate rises. $SPCX is the limiting case of this arithmetic. Its 2025 results — $18.7 billion of revenue against a $4.9 billion net loss and a $2.6 billion operating loss — establish that essentially none of its ~$2 trillion valuation rests on present earnings. The value is entirely terminal: orbital data centers, Mars, a Starlink and AI build-out whose returns, if they arrive, arrive in the 2030s. That is the profile a rising discount rate punishes most, and a *series* of hikes punishes geometrically. An event repriced once; a cycle reprices continuously, meeting-by-meeting, for as long as the dot plot stays elevated. The 10-year Treasury pressing 5% — its highest since 2023 — is the market already writing that reprice into the risk-free rate against which every one of those distant SpaceX cash flows is measured.

### The dot plot is the real event, and the arithmetic is unfriendly

The Summary of Economic Projections released at 2:00 PM is where the cycle-versus-event question resolves in public. The reference points are specific. The June dot plot implied a single 2026 hike, to roughly 3.8%. Since then, the August CPI and a run of firm data have moved futures traders to price two quarter-point increases by year-end. A median dot above 4.00% today signals a follow-on hike is live; a median at exactly 4.00% implies a pause. Given a starting rate the Fed already deems insufficiently restrictive, the burden of proof sits with the doves — and Bank of America, the most hawkish major house, holds to a call for three increases, arguing that after Jackson Hole "the onus is now on Warsh to deliver." For a stock priced on terminal cash flows, the difference between "one and done" and "two more coming" is the difference between a survivable afternoon and a regime change. The base case, on the current data, tilts toward the dots keeping December alive.

### The chair's own doctrine forecloses the dovish escape

Kevin Warsh built this expectation himself. His Jackson Hole keynote — that the summer's better readings "do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved," and that the Fed must be confident inflation is moving to target "clearly and at sufficient speed. Otherwise, we have work to do" — raised the bar for standing pat by explicitly privileging trends over "isolated data points." That construction is a trap for the bulls: it means a single soft data point cannot rescue them, because the chair has pre-committed to looking through exactly that. His refusal to provide forward guidance, preferring the committee have a "good family fight" over the data, removes the other dovish exit — there will be no soothing promise that this is the last hike, because he has spent his tenure declining to make such promises. A new chair, in his first geopolitical crisis, whose credibility rests on the sentence markets already believe, has every institutional incentive to hike and let the dots speak hawkishly. The dovish surprise the bull case needs is the one outcome the chair has spent two months making unavailable.

### The energy shock the Fed is hiking into cannot be fixed by hiking

The inflation forcing today's decision is, in significant part, supply-driven — and that is worse for $SPCX, not better, because it means the pressure does not fade on the Fed's schedule. The Saudi East-West Pipeline, capacity 7 million barrels a day and the kingdom's principal route for bypassing the blockaded Strait of Hormuz, remains shut after a drone strike, with repairs estimated at three to five weeks. That timeline spans today's meeting, the September CPI, and the October FOMC. Diesel sits at a record $6.00 a gallon; that cost is embedded in the freight of every trucked good in the country and reaches core inflation with a lag not yet in the printed data. As one strategist put it plainly, hiking is "a high-sacrifice-ratio, poorly targeted tool" against inflation whose sources are tariffs, the AI build-out, and an oil shock — none of which a policy rate touches. The consequence for the bear case is structural: the Fed cannot declare victory in October because the thing driving the numbers has a supply line under attack, so the hawkish framing persists into the next meeting, and the duration pressure on $SPCX persists with it. The energy shock is not a passing headwind for the stock; it is the mechanism that keeps the Fed hawkish through the whole window.

## Part II: The Launch — Ordinary Risk, Extraordinary Dependence

### The date is already slipping, which is itself information

The bull thesis leans heavily on Flight 14 as the catalyst that reframes everything. The first problem is that the flight keeps receding. The FAA operations advisory lists a NET of September 18, but SpaceX's own announcement and the public launch trackers now show NET September 22 — the date has already moved, and moved past the September options expiry it was expected to electrify. A slipping launch is a slow-motion version of a disappointing one: no telemetry, no narrative fuel, and a market left alone with the supply math and the Fed. Every additional day of slip erodes the one catalyst the bulls hold and pushes it further from the fortnight in which the stock most needs it.

### The mission is genuinely hard, and the program's own record says so

When it flies, Flight 14 attempts a profile of real difficulty: the first orbital insertion of the vehicle, the first deployment of operational Starlink V3 satellites — 26 of them, per the mission profile — a controlled booster splashdown, a deorbit burn, and reentry, on the third flight of the Block 3 generation. This is not a graduation lap. Starship's Block 3 predecessors produced failures in their opening attempts, and the broader program's history is a sequence of vehicles lost on the path to each new capability — orbital velocity first reached only for the vehicle to break up on reentry, booster and ship recoveries achieved only after repeated losses. A program built on iterate-through-failure is admirable engineering and treacherous event-risk: the base rate for a novel-milestone flight is not the ~90% the equity behaves as if it assumes. Prediction markets that assigned a 0.9 probability of the stock finishing higher into an earlier test window are the tell — the enthusiasm has already been priced, which means a merely-nominal outcome disappoints and an anomaly is not cushioned.

### Even a clean flight cannot answer the thing that actually threatens the stock

Here is the asymmetry the launch cannot escape: a flawless Flight 14 proves *capability*, but the stock's problem this week is not capability — it is the *discount rate*. A confirmed V3 deployment demonstrates the technology the orbital-compute story depends on, and it is a genuine achievement. It does not lower the 10-year, un-hike the Fed, cool the September CPI, or shrink the December supply wall. The 2021–22 cohort was full of companies that executed flawlessly while their multiples were cut in half, because execution answers the numerator and a tightening cycle attacks the denominator. A clean launch into a hawkish-Fed tape caps lower and fades faster than the same launch would have in an easy-money regime — which is why even the bull case's best outcome is a bounded prize against an unbounded macro liability.

## Part III: The Supply — A Wall the Narrative Cannot Talk Away

### The float is thin, and thin floats fall as fast as they rise

$SPCX's tradable float is a small fraction of shares outstanding — the mechanical property that let the stock melt up on its debut is the same property that gaps it down when supply arrives without matching demand. Elon Musk holds 49% of the equity and 84% of voting control; a large majority of shares sit with insiders and pre-IPO holders whose lockups release on a schedule. In a low-float name, price is set at the margin, and the margin is about to be flooded.

### The tranche arrives on schedule, into the weakest possible tape

A lockup tranche of up to roughly 328 million shares becomes eligible around September 24. It lands after the Fed, after the launch (if the launch even flies by then, which the slip now calls into question), and into whatever risk appetite survives the week. The precedent is not reassuring: prior 2026 tranches were sold, not bought, on release, and the pattern across the year's unlocks has been to sell the unlock and decline to buy the dip with conviction. This tranche is several times larger than the one the bulls celebrated absorbing earlier in the month, and it clears into a market that will have just been told rates are rising for the foreseeable future. Supply is only a problem when demand is impaired; the Fed's job today is, in effect, to impair demand for exactly this kind of asset.

### The wall behind the wall

The tranche is not the end of the supply schedule; it is the middle of it. Additional tranches follow through October, and the 180-day expiry on December 8 releases the largest block of all — inheriting, by most reconstructions, the shares that failed to release in August when the stock was not sufficiently above its IPO price. Every sophisticated holder facing that December wall knows the same thing: the last clean exit is strength, and strength is scarce in a hiking cycle. That knowledge is itself bearish, because it caps every rally — each move toward the mid-150s meets holders for whom the approach of December makes selling into strength the rational act. The supply overhang does not need panic to press the tape. It needs only holders who can do arithmetic.

### The rebalance cuts both ways, and mostly the wrong way for the bulls

The Nasdaq-100 quarterly rebalance implements at the September 18 close, and the bull case treats the associated index buying as a tailwind. It is real, but it is bounded and one-time: a mechanical, price-insensitive buy that fires once, at a single close, and is gone. It does nothing about the 328-million-share tranche six days later, nothing about the December wall, and nothing about the discount rate. Index-anticipation flows also front-run the event, meaning much of whatever lift it provides has already been absorbed into the tape in the days before — some of the stock's stubborn refusal to break lower this week may be that front-running, which will *reverse* once the rebalance passes. A one-session buy against a quarter's worth of scheduled supply is not a rescue; it is a single day of demand in a calendar dominated by supply.

## Part IV: The Valuation — No Floor, and the First Bears With Numbers

### The multiple was never marked to a rate-normal world

At ~65x run-rate sales against mounting losses, $SPCX carries a valuation that assumes both flawless execution and a permanently low cost of capital. The 2021–22 unwind is the relevant precedent: even elite software franchises could not hold 30–40x sales into a hiking cycle, and the median high-multiple casualty surrendered 60–75% peak-to-trough. $SPCX carries roughly double the peak multiple of that era's most expensive survivors, into the first week of a new cycle, having already given back 36% from its June high — which the bulls read as the reset and the bears read as the first leg. The 2025 financials underline the point: $18.7 billion of revenue, a $4.9 billion net loss, a $2.6 billion operating loss, and only $2.6 billion of total equity against $92.1 billion of total assets. This is a balance sheet that requires continuous access to cheap capital, being repriced by a Fed removing exactly that.

### There is no valuation floor at which buyers must appear

The most dangerous structural feature of the stock is the absence of a fundamental floor. With the compute segment's economics undisclosed and launch cadence the only fully audited operating datapoint, a bear market in the narrative has no price-to-earnings, no free-cash-flow yield, and no sum-of-parts at which value investors are compelled to step in. The published analyst range runs from roughly $62 to $450 with almost nothing between $100 and $140 — meaning that if the stock enters that band, there is no mainstream target for a portfolio manager to anchor a defense. Prices move fastest through the regions where no one has planted a flag, and the entire zone beneath the IPO price is exactly that.

### The first bears with specific numbers have arrived inside the void

For most of the stock's public life the bears spoke in adjectives. This month they began publishing coordinates. A top-ranked institutional voice downgraded the stock to Hold and named the levels at which he would buy: $90 to begin, $60 as more attractive — the first published targets inside the analyst void, and a named buyer at $90 both confirms the vacuum above it and installs a magnet within it. A prominent commentator has repeatedly valued the equity at $10–30, calling the Street's premium targets the stuff of "romantic comedies." A competing orbital-launch chief executive — the most credentialed skeptic in the sector, and one whose business benefits from enthusiasm — called space valuations "completely untethered to reality" and declined to defend the complex's prices at all. And the most respected pattern-matcher for bubbles examined the bearish options on the name, called them "tempting," and chose to express his AI-complex skepticism elsewhere, through a different ticker — a pointed abstention from defending this one. When the numbers being published are $90, $60, $30, and $10, and the current price is $145, the debate is no longer whether the stock is expensive. It is how far below the IPO price fair value sits.

## Part V: The Reflexive Loop, Running in Reverse

The flywheel that carried $SPCX up runs equally well backward, and the bear case is in large part the observation that its supports are load-bearing on one another. The narrative funded the valuation; the valuation funded the capital raises; the capital raises funded the build-out; the build-out justified the narrative. Remove the cheap capital — which is precisely what a hiking cycle does — and each turn of the wheel weakens the next. A rising discount rate compresses the multiple; a compressed multiple raises the cost of the next capital raise for a company that lost $4.9 billion last year; a harder capital environment slows the build-out that the narrative requires; a slowing build-out invites the sell-side downgrades that the analyst void makes violent; the downgrades deepen the multiple compression. Layer onto that the AI-complex reappraisal already underway — the public slowdown pledges from frontier labs, the shelved AI IPO, the Washington guardrail talk — and the marginal buyer of a 65x-sales AI-infrastructure story is stepping back at the exact moment the supply calendar needs him most. None of these is a catastrophe. Each is an ordinary consequence of the others, which is what makes the loop, once it begins turning down, difficult to arrest.

A stock that must repeatedly prove a narrative it cannot fully document, against supply it cannot hide, on a launch schedule it keeps missing, into a cost of capital it cannot control, is a stock being asked to do the one thing a 65x-sales valuation makes hardest — justify itself without cheap money.

## Part VII: What Would Break This Bear Case

The exits, marked honestly, because a case that cannot state its own falsification is not analysis:

- **A dovish dot.** A hike paired with a median dot at exactly 4.00% and a presser framing the move as sufficient would strip the cycle of its forward momentum and hand the duration bid back to the bulls. This is the single most important line to watch at 2:00 PM.
- **A hold.** Low-probability at ~91–93% priced against, but not impossible — Goldman Sachs has called a September hike "very unlikely" and expects a hold through year-end. A hold would detonate the pain trade upward and this document would retire within the hour.
- **A clean, on-time Flight 14.** A confirmed V3 deployment on schedule would neutralize the launch leg and likely carry the stock toward the mid-160s — though, per Part II, it would not touch the discount-rate leg, so it caps the bear case rather than killing it.
- **A fast pipeline restart and Middle East de-escalation.** A phased Aramco restart plus a credible Hormuz arrangement would cool the energy impulse, soften the October CPI, and let the Fed pause — the macro leg's clearest off-ramp.
- **Insider restraint.** If the Form 4 window through October shows minimal selling despite liquid dates, the supply-overhang leg weakens materially, and the informed-money referendum reads bullish into December.

None of these is in evidence this morning. The dots are more likely elevated than flat on the current data; the launch is slipping rather than confirming; the pipeline is weeks from repair; and the supply calendar is fixed. Until one of these exits appears, the ordinary reading of the week stands: a duration asset priced for cheap money, meeting the end of cheap money, with a wall of its own stock arriving behind the news.

## The Close

The bear case for $SPCX does not depend on the rocket exploding, the war widening, or the fraud being uncovered. It depends only on the ordinary things happening in their ordinary way: a hike into an already-insufficient rate, a dot plot that keeps the next one live, an energy shock that outlasts the meeting, a launch that carries its normal risk and has already begun to slip, a tranche that arrives on time, a wall that waits in December, and a valuation with no floor beneath it being asked to stand without the cheap capital that built it. The stock has already fallen 36% pricing the fear of these things. The bear case is that the fear was correct and early, and that today — at 2:00 PM, when the discount rate on every distant SpaceX cash flow is reset upward by the one institution that can — is when the ordinary begins to be paid.
